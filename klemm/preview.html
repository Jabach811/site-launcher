About

Thirty-eight years. One town. One standard.

The story of Tracy's independent broker — and the small team that outworks the big franchises.

Jack Klemm · Broker/Owner

Since 1988, the constant in a changing market

Jack Klemm got his real estate license in 1988, when Tracy was a farm town of 35,000 and the Altamont commute was a curiosity. He's since guided the market through every boom, bust, and building spree — nearly 4,000 closed transactions worth of them.

In November 2008 — the depth of the housing crash, when franchises were fleeing — Jack bet on Tracy and opened his own independent brokerage on 11th Street. Klemm Real Estate has been downtown ever since.

He holds the GRI and CRS designations — earned by fewer than 4% of REALTORS® nationwide — and has received the Central Valley Association of REALTORS® Master's Award every single year for 25 years.

The Commute, Honestly

Yes, there's a commute. Here's how people actually do it.

The ACE Train

Board in downtown Tracy or Lathrop, work or sleep your way to Pleasanton, Fremont, or San Jose. No white knuckles on the 580.

I-205 → I-580

The classic drive over the Altamont. Leave early, podcast up. Many commuters do 2–3 office days and remote the rest.

Hybrid Reality

Post-2020, most of Jack's Bay Area buyers commute 2–3 days a week — which changes the whole equation on what "too far" means.

Mountain House, California

California's newest city — and one of its fastest-growing. First exit after the Altamont, top-rated schools, and a village plan that actually works. Jack has sold here since the first homes went up.

~$963KMedian Sale Price

The shortest commute in the Valley — priced accordingly

Mountain House sits right at the base of the Altamont: it's the closest San Joaquin address to the Bay Area, and the market knows it. You pay a premium over Tracy — roughly $290K at the median — for newer housing stock, K-8 village schools, and that first-exit commute.

The nuance right now: resale homes are seeing price cuts while brand-new construction keeps pulling buyers. If you're selling a resale here, pricing against the builders is the whole strategy. If you're buying, that tension is your leverage — and Jack has negotiated both sides of it.

Where patience is a negotiating strategy

Tracy Hills is the newest large-scale growth in Tracy — and because builder inventory competes with early resales, homes here sit longer than the citywide average. For buyers, that's leverage: incentives, rate buydowns, and price flexibility that don't exist downtown. For sellers, it means positioning against the builder's model home down the street.

What Jack tells people about Tracy

Tracy isn't a suburb pretending to be a town — it was a town first. There's a working downtown on 10th and 11th Streets (Jack's office included), established neighborhoods with mature trees, and new master-planned growth on the edges. Downtown-area homes move in under three weeks; brand-new construction in Tracy Hills takes longer and negotiates softer.

Buyers here split roughly into two camps: Bay Area commuters trading equity for square footage, and Valley families moving up. Knowing which camp you're negotiating against is half the game — and after nearly 4,000 local sales, Jack usually knows the other agent, too.
